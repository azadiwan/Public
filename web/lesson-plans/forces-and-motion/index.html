Forces & motion lesson plan

A ready-to-teach science lesson for 3rd–8th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Aligned to NGSS MS-PS2-2 · PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, PDF, Word

Hook

Why does a soccer ball stop rolling even if nobody touches it?

Learning objectives

  • Analyze a force is a push or pull; forces have size and direction.
  • Apply key vocabulary (force, friction, inertia) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Evaluate solutions or claims about forces & motion using evidence.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up & hook · 5 min
    • Why does a soccer ball stop rolling even if nobody touches it?
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Record predictions/questions on the class chart.
  2. Mini-lesson (I do) · 11 min
    • A force is a push or pull; forces have size and direction.
    • Balanced forces cause no change in motion; unbalanced forces change speed or direction.
    • Friction opposes motion; gravity pulls objects toward Earth.
  3. Guided practice (We do) · 9 min
    • Model: What force slows a sliding book?
    • Together: If forces on an object are balanced, what happens to its motion?
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  4. Independent practice (You do) · 11 min
    • 1. What force slows a sliding book?
    • 2. If forces on an object are balanced, what happens to its motion?
    • 3. Which needs more force to accelerate: an empty or full cart?
  5. Check for understanding · 5 min
    • Quick check: Which needs more force to accelerate: an empty or full cart?
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Closure & exit ticket · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: Explain a rocket launch using Newton's third law.
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • A force is a push or pull; forces have size and direction.
  • Balanced forces cause no change in motion; unbalanced forces change speed or direction.
  • Friction opposes motion; gravity pulls objects toward Earth.
  • Newton's laws: inertia, F = m × a, and equal-and-opposite reactions.

Project idea

Driving question: Design a vehicle that protects an egg passenger in a crash.

Vocabulary

  • force: a push or pull
  • friction: a force that resists motion between surfaces
  • inertia: an object's resistance to change in motion
  • net force: the combined effect of all forces

Check for understanding

  1. What force slows a sliding book?
  2. If forces on an object are balanced, what happens to its motion?
  3. Which needs more force to accelerate: an empty or full cart?
