Main idea & supporting details lesson plan

A ready-to-teach reading lesson for 2nd–5th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Open & customize free →Matching worksheet

Aligned to CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RI.4.2 · PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, PDF, Word

Hook

If you could tell a friend about this article in just one sentence, what would you say?

Learning objectives

  • Explain the main idea is what the text is mostly about.
  • Apply key vocabulary (main idea, supporting detail, summary) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Compare solutions or claims about main idea & supporting details using evidence.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up & hook · 5 min
    • If you could tell a friend about this article in just one sentence, what would you say?
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Record predictions/questions on the class chart.
  2. Mini-lesson (I do) · 11 min
    • The main idea is what the text is mostly about.
    • Supporting details give facts, examples, or reasons that explain the main idea.
    • Titles, headings, and first/last sentences often hint at the main idea.
  3. Guided practice (We do) · 9 min
    • Model: What's the difference between topic and main idea?
    • Together: Where can you often find the main idea?
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  4. Independent practice (You do) · 11 min
    • 1. What's the difference between topic and main idea?
    • 2. Where can you often find the main idea?
    • 3. Why are supporting details important?
  5. Check for understanding · 5 min
    • Quick check: Why are supporting details important?
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Closure & exit ticket · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: Why are supporting details important?
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • The main idea is what the text is mostly about.
  • Supporting details give facts, examples, or reasons that explain the main idea.
  • Titles, headings, and first/last sentences often hint at the main idea.
  • A good summary states the main idea plus key details in your own words.

Project idea

Driving question: Create a 'kid news' bulletin summarizing real articles for younger students.

Vocabulary

  • main idea: the most important point
  • supporting detail: information that explains the main idea
  • summary: a short retelling of the most important parts
  • topic: the subject of a text in a word or two

Check for understanding

  1. What's the difference between topic and main idea?
  2. Where can you often find the main idea?
  3. Why are supporting details important?
