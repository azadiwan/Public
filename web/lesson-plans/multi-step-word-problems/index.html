Multi-step word problems lesson plan

A ready-to-teach test prep lesson for 3rd–8th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Hook

A field trip costs $12 per student plus a $60 bus fee. 25 students go. What's the total?

Learning objectives

  • Analyze read twice: first for the story, second for the numbers and the question.
  • Apply key vocabulary (key words, extraneous information, reasonable) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Evaluate solutions or claims about Multi-step word problems using evidence.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up & hook · 5 min
    • A field trip costs $12 per student plus a $60 bus fee. 25 students go. What's the total?
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Record predictions/questions on the class chart.
  2. Mini-lesson (I do) · 11 min
    • Read twice: first for the story, second for the numbers and the question.
    • Identify what's asked and underline key information; cross out extra information.
    • Plan the steps: draw a model, write an equation, or make a table.
  3. Guided practice (We do) · 9 min
    • Model: Solve the field trip problem from the hook.
    • Together: Maria has 48 stickers and gives 1/4 away, then buys 10. How many now?
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  4. Independent practice (You do) · 11 min
    • 1. Solve the field trip problem from the hook.
    • 2. Maria has 48 stickers and gives 1/4 away, then buys 10. How many now?
    • 3. Why should you estimate first?
  5. Check for understanding · 5 min
    • Quick check: Why should you estimate first?
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Closure & exit ticket · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: Why should you estimate first?
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • Read twice: first for the story, second for the numbers and the question.
  • Identify what's asked and underline key information; cross out extra information.
  • Plan the steps: draw a model, write an equation, or make a table.
  • Check that your answer is reasonable and labeled with units.

Project idea

Driving question: Write a set of original multi-step problems about our school to challenge another class.

Vocabulary

  • key words: clue words that suggest an operation
  • extraneous information: details not needed to solve
  • reasonable: makes sense in context
  • estimate: a close approximate answer
