Spanish: family & descriptions lesson plan

A ready-to-teach world languages lesson for 3rd–8th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Aligned to ACTFL Presentational Novice · PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, PDF, Word

Hook

Show a photo of your family (or a famous family) and describe them — in Spanish!

Learning objectives

  • Analyze family words: madre, padre, hermano/a, abuelo/a, tío/a, primo/a.
  • Apply key vocabulary (la madre, el hermano, alto/a) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Evaluate solutions or claims about family & descriptions using evidence.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up & hook · 5 min
    • Show a photo of your family (or a famous family) and describe them — in Spanish!
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Record predictions/questions on the class chart.
  2. Mini-lesson (I do) · 11 min
    • Family words: madre, padre, hermano/a, abuelo/a, tío/a, primo/a.
    • Possessives: mi, tu, su (mi hermana, tus primos).
    • Describe with ser + adjective: Mi padre es alto y simpático.
  3. Guided practice (We do) · 9 min
    • Model: Translate: my sister is funny.
    • Together: What is the feminine of 'alto'?
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  4. Independent practice (You do) · 11 min
    • 1. Translate: my sister is funny.
    • 2. What is the feminine of 'alto'?
    • 3. What does 'mis abuelos' mean?
  5. Check for understanding · 5 min
    • Quick check: What does 'mis abuelos' mean?
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Closure & exit ticket · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: What does 'mis abuelos' mean?
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • Family words: madre, padre, hermano/a, abuelo/a, tío/a, primo/a.
  • Possessives: mi, tu, su (mi hermana, tus primos).
  • Describe with ser + adjective: Mi padre es alto y simpático.
  • Adjectives agree in gender and number: alto/alta/altos/altas.

Project idea

Driving question: Create a family tree poster with Spanish descriptions and present it to the class.

Vocabulary

  • la madre: mother
  • el hermano: brother
  • alto/a: tall
  • simpático/a: nice

Check for understanding

  2. What is the feminine of 'alto'?
  3. What does 'mis abuelos' mean?
