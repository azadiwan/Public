Ratios & proportional relationships lesson plan

A ready-to-teach math lesson for 6th–7th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Aligned to CCSS.MATH.6.RP.A.3 · PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, PDF, Word

Hook

A recipe for 4 people needs 2 cups of rice. How much for our whole class?

Learning objectives

  • Analyze a ratio compares two quantities (3:2, 3 to 2, or 3/2).
  • Apply key vocabulary (ratio, unit rate, proportion) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Evaluate solutions or claims about ratios & proportional relationships using evidence.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up & hook · 5 min
    • A recipe for 4 people needs 2 cups of rice. How much for our whole class?
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Record predictions/questions on the class chart.
  2. Mini-lesson (I do) · 11 min
    • A ratio compares two quantities (3:2, 3 to 2, or 3/2).
    • A unit rate tells how much of one quantity per 1 unit of another (e.g. $3 per pound).
    • Equivalent ratios scale both quantities by the same factor; ratio tables and double number lines organize them.
  3. Guided practice (We do) · 9 min
    • Model: A car travels 150 miles in 3 hours. What is the unit rate?
    • Together: Are 4:6 and 10:15 equivalent?
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  4. Independent practice (You do) · 11 min
    • 1. A car travels 150 miles in 3 hours. What is the unit rate?
    • 2. Are 4:6 and 10:15 equivalent?
    • 3. If 5 notebooks cost $12.50, how much do 8 cost?
  5. Check for understanding · 5 min
    • Quick check: If 5 notebooks cost $12.50, how much do 8 cost?
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Closure & exit ticket · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: Does y = 4x show a proportional relationship? What is k?
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • A ratio compares two quantities (3:2, 3 to 2, or 3/2).
  • A unit rate tells how much of one quantity per 1 unit of another (e.g. $3 per pound).
  • Equivalent ratios scale both quantities by the same factor; ratio tables and double number lines organize them.
  • A proportional relationship has a constant ratio and graphs as a straight line through the origin.

Project idea

Driving question: Which grocery store gives our families the best value? Compare unit prices and recommend a shopping plan.

Vocabulary

  • ratio: a comparison of two quantities
  • unit rate: a rate with a denominator of 1
  • proportion: an equation stating two ratios are equal
  • constant of proportionality: the constant k in y = kx
