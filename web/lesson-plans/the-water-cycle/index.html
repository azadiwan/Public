The water cycle lesson plan

A ready-to-teach science lesson for 2nd–5th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Hook

The water you drank today might have been dinosaur drinking water. How?

Learning objectives

  • Explain earth's water is constantly recycled through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection.
  • Apply key vocabulary (evaporation, condensation, precipitation) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Compare solutions or claims about the water cycle using evidence.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up & hook · 5 min
    • The water you drank today might have been dinosaur drinking water. How?
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Record predictions/questions on the class chart.
  2. Mini-lesson (I do) · 11 min
    • Earth's water is constantly recycled through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection.
    • The sun's energy drives evaporation.
    • Water vapor cools and condenses into clouds.
  3. Guided practice (We do) · 9 min
    • Model: What powers the water cycle?
    • Together: How do clouds form?
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  4. Independent practice (You do) · 11 min
    • 1. What powers the water cycle?
    • 2. How do clouds form?
    • 3. Name two types of precipitation.
  5. Check for understanding · 5 min
    • Quick check: Name two types of precipitation.
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Closure & exit ticket · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: Where does runoff go?
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • Earth's water is constantly recycled through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection.
  • The sun's energy drives evaporation.
  • Water vapor cools and condenses into clouds.
  • Precipitation returns water to oceans, lakes, and groundwater.

Project idea

Driving question: How can our school collect and reuse rainwater for the garden?

Vocabulary

  • evaporation: liquid water becoming vapor
  • condensation: vapor cooling into droplets
  • precipitation: water falling as rain, snow, sleet, or hail
  • runoff: water flowing over land into bodies of water

Check for understanding

  1. What powers the water cycle?
  2. How do clouds form?
  3. Name two types of precipitation.
