Ecosystems & food webs lesson plan

A ready-to-teach science lesson for 4th–7th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Aligned to NGSS MS-LS2-3 · PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, PDF, Word

Hook

When wolves returned to Yellowstone, rivers changed course. How?

Learning objectives

  • Analyze energy flows from the sun to producers, then to consumers and decomposers.
  • Apply key vocabulary (producer, consumer, decomposer) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Evaluate solutions or claims about ecosystems & food webs using evidence.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up & hook · 5 min
    • When wolves returned to Yellowstone, rivers changed course. How?
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Record predictions/questions on the class chart.
  2. Mini-lesson (I do) · 11 min
    • Energy flows from the sun to producers, then to consumers and decomposers.
    • A food chain shows one path of energy; a food web shows many connected chains.
    • Only about 10% of energy passes to the next level.
  3. Guided practice (We do) · 9 min
    • Model: What is the original source of energy in most ecosystems?
    • Together: Give an example of a decomposer.
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  4. Independent practice (You do) · 11 min
    • 1. What is the original source of energy in most ecosystems?
    • 2. Give an example of a decomposer.
    • 3. What might happen if all the rabbits disappeared from a meadow?
  5. Check for understanding · 5 min
    • Quick check: What might happen if all the rabbits disappeared from a meadow?
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Closure & exit ticket · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: Why are there fewer top predators than producers?
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • Energy flows from the sun to producers, then to consumers and decomposers.
  • A food chain shows one path of energy; a food web shows many connected chains.
  • Only about 10% of energy passes to the next level.
  • Changing one population ripples through the whole ecosystem.

Project idea

Driving question: An invasive species arrived in our local park. Model its impact and propose a plan to protect the ecosystem.

Vocabulary

  • producer: an organism that makes its own food
  • consumer: an organism that eats other organisms
  • decomposer: breaks down dead matter
  • trophic level: a feeding position in a food chain

Check for understanding

  1. What is the original source of energy in most ecosystems?
  2. Give an example of a decomposer.
  3. What might happen if all the rabbits disappeared from a meadow?
