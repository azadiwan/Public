Engineering a water filter lesson plan

A ready-to-teach problem-based / stem lesson for 3rd–8th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Aligned to NGSS MS-ETS1-2 · PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, PDF, Word

Hook

Nearly 2 billion people drink unsafe water. Could you design a filter with sand and charcoal?

Learning objectives

  • Analyze the engineering design process: ask, imagine, plan, create, test, improve.
  • Apply key vocabulary (filtration, constraint, criteria) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Evaluate solutions or claims about engineering a water filter using evidence.
  • Collaborate to design and defend a solution to a real-world problem.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Launch the driving question · 5 min
    • Nearly 2 billion people drink unsafe water. Could you design a filter with sand and charcoal?
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Driving question: Design the most effective, lowest-cost water filter for a community with dirty water.
  2. Need-to-know mini-lesson · 8 min
    • The engineering design process: ask, imagine, plan, create, test, improve.
    • Filtration layers (gravel, sand, charcoal) remove particles of different sizes.
    • Measure results: clarity, flow rate, cost.
  3. Team investigation · 14 min
    • Teams list what they know and need to know.
    • Divide research roles (researcher, recorder, designer, presenter).
    • Investigate: The engineering design process: ask, imagine, plan, create, test, improve.
  4. Build & test a solution · 9 min
    • Model: Why use several filter layers?
    • Together: What's a constraint in this project?
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  5. Gallery share & feedback · 5 min
    • Quick check: How could you measure filter success?
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Reflection · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: How could you measure filter success?
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • The engineering design process: ask, imagine, plan, create, test, improve.
  • Filtration layers (gravel, sand, charcoal) remove particles of different sizes.
  • Measure results: clarity, flow rate, cost.
  • Real engineers balance constraints like cost, materials, and time.

Project idea

Driving question: Design the most effective, lowest-cost water filter for a community with dirty water.

Vocabulary

  • filtration: separating solids from liquids
  • constraint: a limit on a design
  • criteria: requirements for success
  • iterate: improve through repeated testing

Check for understanding

  1. Why use several filter layers?
  2. What's a constraint in this project?
  3. How could you measure filter success?
