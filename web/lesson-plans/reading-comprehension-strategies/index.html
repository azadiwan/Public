Reading comprehension strategies lesson plan

A ready-to-teach test prep lesson for 3rd–8th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Hook

Here's a passage and 5 questions. Should you read the questions first or the passage first?

Learning objectives

  • Analyze preview questions, then read actively — underline key ideas and circle unfamiliar words.
  • Apply key vocabulary (process of elimination, distractor, text evidence) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Evaluate solutions or claims about reading comprehension strategies using evidence.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up & hook · 5 min
    • Here's a passage and 5 questions. Should you read the questions first or the passage first?
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Record predictions/questions on the class chart.
  2. Mini-lesson (I do) · 11 min
    • Preview questions, then read actively — underline key ideas and circle unfamiliar words.
    • Use process of elimination: cross out answers that are too broad, too narrow, or not in the text.
    • For evidence-based questions, the answer to Part B must support Part A.
  3. Guided practice (We do) · 9 min
    • Model: What is a distractor?
    • Together: Name two ways to eliminate answer choices.
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  4. Independent practice (You do) · 11 min
    • 1. What is a distractor?
    • 2. Name two ways to eliminate answer choices.
    • 3. What's the link between Part A and Part B questions?
  5. Check for understanding · 5 min
    • Quick check: What's the link between Part A and Part B questions?
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Closure & exit ticket · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: What's the link between Part A and Part B questions?
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • Preview questions, then read actively — underline key ideas and circle unfamiliar words.
  • Use process of elimination: cross out answers that are too broad, too narrow, or not in the text.
  • For evidence-based questions, the answer to Part B must support Part A.
  • Budget time: roughly 1 minute per question, and return to skipped items.

Project idea

Driving question: Create a class 'strategy playbook' with examples of each test-taking strategy.

Vocabulary

  • process of elimination: ruling out wrong answers
  • distractor: a tempting but wrong choice
  • text evidence: a quote or detail that proves an answer
  • annotate: mark up a text while reading

Check for understanding

  2. Name two ways to eliminate answer choices.
  3. What's the link between Part A and Part B questions?
