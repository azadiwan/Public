The American Revolution lesson plan

A ready-to-teach history lesson for 5th–8th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Aligned to C3 D2.His.14.6-8 · PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, PDF, Word

Hook

Would you risk everything to break away from the most powerful empire on Earth over a tax on tea?

Learning objectives

  • Analyze after the French and Indian War, Britain taxed the colonies (Stamp Act, Townshend Acts, Tea Act) without colonial representation.
  • Apply key vocabulary (taxation without representation, Patriot, Loyalist) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Evaluate solutions or claims about The American Revolution using evidence.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up & hook · 5 min
    • Would you risk everything to break away from the most powerful empire on Earth over a tax on tea?
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Record predictions/questions on the class chart.
  2. Mini-lesson (I do) · 11 min
    • After the French and Indian War, Britain taxed the colonies (Stamp Act, Townshend Acts, Tea Act) without colonial representation.
    • Colonists protested: boycotts, the Boston Tea Party; Britain responded with the Intolerable Acts.
    • Fighting began at Lexington and Concord (1775); the Declaration of Independence was adopted July 4, 1776.
  3. Guided practice (We do) · 9 min
    • Model: Why were colonists angry about the Stamp Act?
    • Together: What happened at the Boston Tea Party?
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  4. Independent practice (You do) · 11 min
    • 1. Why were colonists angry about the Stamp Act?
    • 2. What happened at the Boston Tea Party?
    • 3. When was the Declaration of Independence adopted?
  5. Check for understanding · 5 min
    • Quick check: When was the Declaration of Independence adopted?
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Closure & exit ticket · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: What treaty ended the war?
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • After the French and Indian War, Britain taxed the colonies (Stamp Act, Townshend Acts, Tea Act) without colonial representation.
  • Colonists protested: boycotts, the Boston Tea Party; Britain responded with the Intolerable Acts.
  • Fighting began at Lexington and Concord (1775); the Declaration of Independence was adopted July 4, 1776.
  • With French help, the colonists won at Yorktown (1781); the Treaty of Paris (1783) recognized independence.

Project idea

Driving question: Was the American Revolution inevitable? Build an evidence-based museum exhibit to argue your position.

Vocabulary

  • taxation without representation: being taxed by a government you have no vote in
  • Patriot: a colonist who supported independence
  • Loyalist: a colonist loyal to Britain
  • boycott: refusing to buy goods as protest

Check for understanding

  1. Why were colonists angry about the Stamp Act?
  2. What happened at the Boston Tea Party?
  3. When was the Declaration of Independence adopted?
