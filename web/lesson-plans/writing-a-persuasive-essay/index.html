Writing a persuasive essay lesson plan

A ready-to-teach english / ela lesson for 4th–8th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Aligned to CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.W.6.1 · PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, PDF, Word

Hook

Should our school have a four-day week? Convince me in one sentence.

Learning objectives

  • Analyze a persuasive essay states a clear claim (thesis) and tries to convince readers.
  • Apply key vocabulary (claim, evidence, counterclaim) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Evaluate solutions or claims about writing a persuasive essay using evidence.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up & hook · 5 min
    • Should our school have a four-day week? Convince me in one sentence.
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Record predictions/questions on the class chart.
  2. Mini-lesson (I do) · 11 min
    • A persuasive essay states a clear claim (thesis) and tries to convince readers.
    • Support the claim with reasons and evidence: facts, statistics, examples, expert quotes.
    • Address a counterclaim and rebut it to strengthen your argument.
  3. Guided practice (We do) · 9 min
    • Model: What is a claim?
    • Together: Name two types of evidence.
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  4. Independent practice (You do) · 11 min
    • 1. What is a claim?
    • 2. Name two types of evidence.
    • 3. Why include a counterclaim?
  5. Check for understanding · 5 min
    • Quick check: Why include a counterclaim?
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Closure & exit ticket · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: Write a thesis for: 'Should homework be banned?'
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • A persuasive essay states a clear claim (thesis) and tries to convince readers.
  • Support the claim with reasons and evidence: facts, statistics, examples, expert quotes.
  • Address a counterclaim and rebut it to strengthen your argument.
  • Use transitions and a strong conclusion with a call to action.

Project idea

Driving question: Write a persuasive letter to the principal proposing one change that would improve our school.

Vocabulary

  • claim: the main argument
  • evidence: facts or examples that support a claim
  • counterclaim: an opposing argument
  • rebuttal: a response that disproves a counterclaim

Check for understanding

  1. What is a claim?
  2. Name two types of evidence.
  3. Why include a counterclaim?
