Fractions: equivalent fractions lesson plan

A ready-to-teach math lesson for 3rd–5th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Open & customize free →

Aligned to CCSS.MATH.4.NF.A.1 · PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, PDF, Word

Hook

You and a friend each order a pizza. You eat 2 of 4 slices; they eat 4 of 8 slices. Who ate more?

Learning objectives

  • Explain a fraction names equal parts of a whole: the denominator counts the parts, the numerator counts the parts we have.
  • Apply key vocabulary (numerator, denominator, equivalent) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Compare solutions or claims about equivalent fractions using evidence.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up & hook · 5 min
    • You and a friend each order a pizza. You eat 2 of 4 slices; they eat 4 of 8 slices. Who ate more?
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Record predictions/questions on the class chart.
  2. Mini-lesson (I do) · 11 min
    • A fraction names equal parts of a whole: the denominator counts the parts, the numerator counts the parts we have.
    • Equivalent fractions name the same amount even though the numbers differ (1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8).
    • Multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same number to make an equivalent fraction.
  3. Guided practice (We do) · 9 min
    • Model: Write a fraction equivalent to 3/4.
    • Together: Is 2/6 equivalent to 1/3? Explain.
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  4. Independent practice (You do) · 11 min
    • 1. Write a fraction equivalent to 3/4.
    • 2. Is 2/6 equivalent to 1/3? Explain.
    • 3. Fill in: 5/10 = ?/2
  5. Check for understanding · 5 min
    • Quick check: Fill in: 5/10 = ?/2
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Closure & exit ticket · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: Draw a model showing 2/3 = 4/6.
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • A fraction names equal parts of a whole: the denominator counts the parts, the numerator counts the parts we have.
  • Equivalent fractions name the same amount even though the numbers differ (1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8).
  • Multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same number to make an equivalent fraction.
  • Fraction strips and number lines show equivalence visually — equal lengths mean equal amounts.

Project idea

Driving question: How can we split 3 pizzas fairly among our class so everyone gets an equivalent share?

Vocabulary

  • numerator: the top number; how many parts we have
  • denominator: the bottom number; how many equal parts make the whole
  • equivalent: having the same value
  • simplest form: a fraction whose numerator and denominator share no factor but 1

Check for understanding

  2. Is 2/6 equivalent to 1/3? Explain.
  3. Fill in: 5/10 = ?/2
