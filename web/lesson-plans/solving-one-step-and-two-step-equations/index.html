Solving one-step & two-step equations lesson plan

A ready-to-teach math lesson for 6th–8th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Open & customize free →

Aligned to CCSS.MATH.7.EE.B.4 · PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, PDF, Word

Hook

I'm thinking of a number. Double it and add 5, you get 17. What's my number?

Learning objectives

  • Analyze an equation says two expressions are equal — like a balanced scale.
  • Apply key vocabulary (variable, inverse operation, coefficient) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Evaluate solutions or claims about solving one-step & two-step equations using evidence.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up & hook · 5 min
    • I'm thinking of a number. Double it and add 5, you get 17. What's my number?
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Record predictions/questions on the class chart.
  2. Mini-lesson (I do) · 11 min
    • An equation says two expressions are equal — like a balanced scale.
    • Use inverse operations to isolate the variable: undo addition with subtraction, multiplication with division.
    • Whatever you do to one side, do to the other to keep the balance.
  3. Guided practice (We do) · 9 min
    • Model: Solve: x + 7 = 15
    • Together: Solve: 4y = 28
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  4. Independent practice (You do) · 11 min
    • 1. Solve: x + 7 = 15
    • 2. Solve: 4y = 28
    • 3. Solve: 2n + 5 = 17
  5. Check for understanding · 5 min
    • Quick check: Solve: 2n + 5 = 17
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Closure & exit ticket · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: Write and solve an equation: 3 tickets plus a $4 fee cost $31.
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • An equation says two expressions are equal — like a balanced scale.
  • Use inverse operations to isolate the variable: undo addition with subtraction, multiplication with division.
  • Whatever you do to one side, do to the other to keep the balance.
  • Check a solution by substituting it back into the original equation.

Project idea

Driving question: Plan a class fundraiser: write and solve equations to decide the ticket price that meets our goal.

Vocabulary

  • variable: a letter that stands for an unknown number
  • inverse operation: an operation that undoes another
  • coefficient: the number multiplied by a variable
  • solution: a value that makes an equation true

Check for understanding

  1. Solve: x + 7 = 15
  2. Solve: 4y = 28
  3. Solve: 2n + 5 = 17
