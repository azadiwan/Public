Photosynthesis lesson plan

A ready-to-teach science lesson for 5th–7th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Aligned to NGSS MS-LS1-6 · PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, PDF, Word

Hook

A tiny seed becomes a 2-ton oak tree. Where does all that mass come from?

Learning objectives

  • Analyze plants make their own food (glucose) through photosynthesis.
  • Apply key vocabulary (photosynthesis, chlorophyll, chloroplast) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Evaluate solutions or claims about photosynthesis using evidence.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up & hook · 5 min
    • A tiny seed becomes a 2-ton oak tree. Where does all that mass come from?
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Record predictions/questions on the class chart.
  2. Mini-lesson (I do) · 11 min
    • Plants make their own food (glucose) through photosynthesis.
    • Inputs: carbon dioxide from the air, water from the roots, and energy from sunlight.
    • Outputs: glucose (stored energy) and oxygen released into the air.
  3. Guided practice (We do) · 9 min
    • Model: What three things do plants need for photosynthesis?
    • Together: What gas do plants release?
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  4. Independent practice (You do) · 11 min
    • 1. What three things do plants need for photosynthesis?
    • 2. What gas do plants release?
    • 3. Where in the cell does photosynthesis occur?
  5. Check for understanding · 5 min
    • Quick check: Where in the cell does photosynthesis occur?
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Closure & exit ticket · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: Why would a plant in a dark closet stop growing?
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • Plants make their own food (glucose) through photosynthesis.
  • Inputs: carbon dioxide from the air, water from the roots, and energy from sunlight.
  • Outputs: glucose (stored energy) and oxygen released into the air.
  • Chlorophyll in chloroplasts captures light energy; most of a plant's mass comes from CO₂ in the air.

Project idea

Driving question: How can we design a classroom plant setup that maximizes growth with the least water?

Vocabulary

  • photosynthesis: the process plants use to turn light energy into chemical energy
  • chlorophyll: green pigment that absorbs light
  • chloroplast: the organelle where photosynthesis happens
  • glucose: a sugar that stores energy

Check for understanding

  1. What three things do plants need for photosynthesis?
  2. What gas do plants release?
  3. Where in the cell does photosynthesis occur?
