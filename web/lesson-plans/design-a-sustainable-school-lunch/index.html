Design a sustainable school lunch lesson plan

A ready-to-teach problem-based / stem lesson for 4th–8th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Aligned to NGSS 3-5-ETS1-1 · CCSS Math Practice 4 · PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, PDF, Word

Hook

Our cafeteria throws away about 40 pounds of food every day. Can we fix that?

Learning objectives

  • Analyze define the problem with data: measure and categorize waste.
  • Apply key vocabulary (sustainability, prototype, data) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Evaluate solutions or claims about design a sustainable school lunch using evidence.
  • Collaborate to design and defend a solution to a real-world problem.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Launch the driving question · 5 min
    • Our cafeteria throws away about 40 pounds of food every day. Can we fix that?
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Driving question: How might we cut our cafeteria food waste in half while keeping lunches healthy and popular?
  2. Need-to-know mini-lesson · 8 min
    • Define the problem with data: measure and categorize waste.
    • Research nutrition guidelines, costs, and student preferences.
    • Brainstorm and prototype solutions (menu changes, share tables, composting).
  3. Team investigation · 14 min
    • Teams list what they know and need to know.
    • Divide research roles (researcher, recorder, designer, presenter).
    • Investigate: Define the problem with data: measure and categorize waste.
  4. Build & test a solution · 9 min
    • Model: Why collect data before brainstorming?
    • Together: Who are the stakeholders in school lunch?
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  5. Gallery share & feedback · 5 min
    • Quick check: How will we know if our solution worked?
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Reflection · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: How will we know if our solution worked?
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • Define the problem with data: measure and categorize waste.
  • Research nutrition guidelines, costs, and student preferences.
  • Brainstorm and prototype solutions (menu changes, share tables, composting).
  • Test, measure impact, iterate, and present recommendations to decision-makers.

Project idea

Driving question: How might we cut our cafeteria food waste in half while keeping lunches healthy and popular?

Vocabulary

  • sustainability: meeting needs without harming the future
  • prototype: an early model to test an idea
  • data: facts collected for analysis
  • stakeholder: anyone affected by a decision

Check for understanding

  1. Why collect data before brainstorming?
  2. Who are the stakeholders in school lunch?
  3. How will we know if our solution worked?
