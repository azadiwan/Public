Place value & rounding lesson plan

A ready-to-teach math lesson for 2nd–4th grade. Every block is timed, from the hook to the exit ticket, and slides, a worksheet and an answer key are one click away.

Open & customize free →

Aligned to CCSS.MATH.4.NBT.A.3 · PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, PDF, Word

Hook

A stadium holds 48,562 fans. A reporter says 'about 50,000.' Is that fair?

Learning objectives

  • Explain each digit's value depends on its place: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands….
  • Apply key vocabulary (place value, digit, round) accurately in speaking and writing.
  • Compare solutions or claims about place value & rounding using evidence.

Lesson outline (45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up & hook · 5 min
    • A stadium holds 48,562 fans. A reporter says 'about 50,000.' Is that fair?
    • Think–pair–share: jot an answer, discuss with a partner, share out.
    • Record predictions/questions on the class chart.
  2. Mini-lesson (I do) · 11 min
    • Each digit's value depends on its place: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands…
    • Each place is worth 10 times the place to its right.
    • To round, look at the digit to the right of the rounding place: 5 or more rounds up, 4 or less stays.
  3. Guided practice (We do) · 9 min
    • Model: What is the value of 7 in 3,742?
    • Together: Round 486 to the nearest ten.
    • Students attempt the next step on whiteboards before you reveal it.
  4. Independent practice (You do) · 11 min
    • 1. What is the value of 7 in 3,742?
    • 2. Round 486 to the nearest ten.
    • 3. Round 6,512 to the nearest thousand.
  5. Check for understanding · 5 min
    • Quick check: Round 6,512 to the nearest thousand.
    • Whiteboard response or digital poll — scan for misconceptions.
    • Pull a small group for reteach if needed.
  6. Closure & exit ticket · 4 min
    • Exit ticket: Write 4,000 + 300 + 9 in standard form.
    • Revisit the hook question — what do we know now?
    • Preview what's next.

Key ideas to teach

  • Each digit's value depends on its place: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands…
  • Each place is worth 10 times the place to its right.
  • To round, look at the digit to the right of the rounding place: 5 or more rounds up, 4 or less stays.
  • Rounding helps estimate and check whether answers are reasonable.

Project idea

Driving question: How many books are in our school library? Estimate using rounding, then check.

Vocabulary

  • place value: the value of a digit based on its position
  • digit: any of the symbols 0–9
  • round: to change a number to a nearby, simpler number
  • estimate: a close guess found by rounding

Check for understanding

  1. What is the value of 7 in 3,742?
  2. Round 486 to the nearest ten.
  3. Round 6,512 to the nearest thousand.
